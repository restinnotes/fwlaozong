题目: Inferior Frontal Gyrus Alterations in Freezing of Gait in Parkinson's Disease Without Evidence for Overall Hemispheric Lateralization: A Systematic Review and Activation Likelihood Estimation Meta-Analysis.
年份: 2026  类型: Meta-Analysis; Systematic Review; review-article; Review; Journal Article
摘要: Freezing of gait (FOG) is one of the most debilitating motor symptoms of Parkinson's disease (PD). Prior studies have suggested a potential right-hemispheric preference in the pathophysiology of FOG. Yet, there is limited evidence regarding which brain regions are most consistently associated with FOG and whether these alterations indeed show right-hemispheric lateralization. We aimed to identify brain regions associated with FOG based on multimodal neuroimaging findings and to examine their hemispheric lateralization. A systematic review across multiple neuroimaging modalities was performed, followed by an activation likelihood estimation (ALE) coordinate-based meta-analysis to assess convergent brain alterations in PD patients with FOG (freezers) versus those without FOG (nonfreezers). Hemispheric lateralization was assessed using the distribution of reported stereotactic coordinates. The systematic review included 108 articles, of which 33 were included in the primary ALE meta-analysis. We identified a significant convergent cluster in the right inferior frontal gyrus (IFG) pars opercularis/dorsolateral prefrontal cortex in PD freezers compared with nonfreezers. No evidence of overall hemispheric lateralization was observed based on the distribution of reported coordinates. Our findings identify the right IFG as a consistent neural correlate of FOG, potentially reflecting heightened frontal recruitment, with no clear evidence of hemispheric lateralization. The lack of further consistency in brain regions could reflect the known heterogeneity in the pathophysiology of FOG.

题目: Decoding Parkinson's disease: a systematic review of qEEG biomarkers across clinical domains.
年份: 2026  类型: review-article; Review; Journal Article
摘要: ObjectiveThis systematic review synthesized evidence on quantitative EEG (qEEG) correlates of Parkinson's disease (PD) across five domains: cognition, motor function, pharmacological interventions, neuromodulation, and sleep/non-motor features, evaluating qEEG's potential as a biomarker across these clinical domains.MethodsFollowing PRISMA, we searched PubMed, Embase, Web of Science, and Scopus for studies published from 1991 to 2025. We included studies with ≥10 idiopathic PD patients, resting-state qEEG, and empirical data. Methodological quality was assessed using JBI checklists and AMSTAR.ResultsWe included 89 studies. Spectral slowing - reduced posterior dominant frequency and increased theta power - was the most consistent correlate of cognitive impairment. Connectivity measures (PLV, graph metrics) showed stronger associations with motor dysfunction, gait impairment, and freezing than spectral power alone. qEEG changed with dopaminergic drugs (increased alpha/beta) and neuromodulation (DBS, tDCS, rTMS, neurofeedback). Sleep studies revealed spindle density reductions linked to dementia risk.ConclusionsqEEG is a promising biomarker for PD, with distinct electrophysiological correlates across cognitive, motor, pharmacological, and neuromodulatory domains. However, methodological standardization is needed before clinical use.SignificanceWith standardized protocols and multicenter validation, qEEG could aid diagnosis, progression tracking, and personalized therapy in PD.

摘要: ObjectivesTo evaluate the effectiveness of a dual-target deep brain stimulation (DBS) approach, stimulating both the subthalamic nucleus (STN) and the substantia nigra pars reticulata (SNr), and improvement of gait function and quality of life (QoL) in patients with Parkinson's disease (PD).DesignSystematic review and meta-analysis of crossover randomised controlled trials (RCTs).SettingA systematic search was conducted across PubMed, ScienceDirect and Scopus databases up to October 2025.ParticipantsAdults with advanced PD (disease duration >5 years) experiencing clinically significant and refractory freezing of gait (FoG) despite optimal dopaminergic therapy and standard STN-DBS.InterventionsDual-target DBS stimulation of the STN and SNr compared with conventional STN-only stimulation.Main outcome measuresFoG and QoL.ResultsEight crossover RCTs (n=97) were included. pid: PMID:42983899
题目: Time to Move Upstream? A Framework for Expanding the Focus of Freezing of Gait to Prodromal, Subclinical, and Pre-FOG Stages.
年份: 2026  类型: review-article; Review; Journal Article
摘要: Freezing of gait (FOG) is one of the most disabling motor symptoms of Parkinson's disease, yet a fundamental question remains unresolved: why does FOG occur at any given moment? Most research has focused on studying FOG once it is already manifest. The present work aims to synthesize recent observations and propose a conceptual framework in which FOG reflects a dynamic process unfolding across multiple stages and time scales. Subtle changes in gait dynamics and physiology can precede episodes by several seconds (Stage -1), and experimental paradigms show that individuals without clinically recognized freezing may exhibit brief episodes under sufficiently sensitive conditions (Stage -2). At longer time scales, prospective studies indicate that vulnerability to freezing may be detectable years before onset (Stage -3). This framework reflects the idea that FOG emerges from progressive instability across interacting motor, cognitive, autonomic, and environmental systems, rather than as an isolated event. The proposed framework includes a prodromal stage, a subclinical stage, an immediate prefreezing state, and the freezing episode itself (Stage 0). This framework integrates findings from wearable sensing, physiology, and clinical studies and emphasizes that FOG remains latent in Stage -3, may be elicited only under specific conditions in Stage -2, and becomes manifest as instability evolves toward imminent and overt freezing (Stages -1 and 0, respectively). Reframing FOG as a multistage process that includes an upstream phase raises new questions regarding mechanisms, classification, and intervention, and may, ultimately, inform strategies aimed at predicting and preventing freezing rather than simply reacting to it.

摘要: Postural-locomotor disorders, particularly freezing of gait and postural instability, are among the most disabling symptoms of advanced Parkinson's disease (PD) and respond only partially to dopaminergic therapy. Evidence for virtual reality (VR) approaches in moderate to severe PD remains limited. This prospective randomized controlled open-label exploratory trial evaluated the addition of multisensory passive immersive VR with synchronized plantar tactile stimulation to conventional rehabilitation for gait, balance, and freezing disorders in moderate-to-severe PD (Hoehn and Yahr Stages 2-4). In the study, 80 PD patients were randomized into an experimental group (EXP, n = 40) receiving standard pharmacotherapy and conventional rehabilitation plus 10 sessions of passive immersive VR with synchronized plantar tactile stimulation and a comparison group (COM, n = 40) receiving standard pharmacotherapy and conventional rehabilitation only. Because the VR sessions were added to an otherwise identical program, the trial evaluates the effect of adding this intervention rather than a VR- or tactile-specific effect. Outcomes were assessed at baseline and after 10 days using the Unified Parkinson's Disease Rating Scale (UPDRS), Berg Balance Scale (BBS), and Freezing of Gait Questionnaire (FoG-Q). The EXP group showed significant improvements in the UPDRS "Gait" cluster (an eight-item composite of UPDRS items 2.11, 2.12, 2.13, 3.9, 3.10, 3.11, 3.12, and 3.13, range 0-32; median from 12.5 to 8.0 points, p < 0.001), BBS (from 38.0 to 43.5 points, p < 0.001), and FoG-Q (from 9.0 to 6.5 points, p < 0.001). A formal group × time analysis confirmed a differential treatment effect for every outcome. For the main outcome, the UPDRS "Gait" cluster, the baseline-adjusted between-group difference was -2.48 points (95% CI -3.37 to -1.59; p < 0.001; Hedges' g = 1.16).
